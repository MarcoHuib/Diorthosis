# Spelling and future quality research (2026-10-20)

## Spelling feasibility decision: postpone integration

No spelling package, dictionary or native binary is adopted, downloaded, or added to THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES. No reviewed local Hunspell/Nuspell executable or pinned NL/EN dictionary pair was found in the inspected development paths. There is no executed dictionary-only/hybrid repair comparison, size, latency or memory result. Candidate detection alone would not demonstrate a repair benefit. Lack of independently reviewed subtype coverage also prevents promotion. This is a documented negative integration decision, not a conclusion that a hybrid cannot help.

Under an LGPL path, dynamic-link replacement/relinking and corresponding-source requirements need an explicit packaging design; static Native AOT/native linking can add relinking obligations. Under MPL, covered source modifications/distribution obligations must be assessed. Preserve source, licenses, notices, build scripts and compiler-runtime/ICU obligations for the actual selected artifacts. These are review questions, not a legal approval or a blanket statement that commercial use is forbidden.

A future isolated experiment should pin all assets outside the app and freeze three arms: existing LLM-only, dictionary-only candidate diagnostics, and dictionary candidates passed as untrusted hints to the same local LLM. The validator remains authoritative and never auto-replaces dictionary suggestions. Include NL compounds, separable verbs, participles, homophones, d/t/dt, diacritics, typos, uncommon correct words, regional forms, names, software terms and alphanumeric IDs; EN variants/contractions and domain words; hundreds of genuinely varied clean passages. Count independently adjudicated incremental repairs, misses, incorrect flags, unsafe proposals, package bytes, native startup, per-case latency and sampled peak process RAM. A token being listed is not contextual correctness (e.g. valid-word homophones). Decision: retain LLM-only default until measured incremental benefit, licenses and portable packaging justify another component.

## Optional edit voting, verification and fine-tuning

[Goto, Sakai and Watanabe (BEA 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.bea-1.60/) propose edit-level majority voting over candidates from one model; the paper's abstract reports multilingual experiments without Dutch. This does not establish benefit for Diorthosis, our models or our native runtime. Proposed experiment: one greedy candidate versus independently sampled candidates with fixed seeds, aligned atomic edit/dependency groups and strict majority thresholds; reject overlapping/dependency-incomplete combinations. Measure false-positive reduction, lost repairs, correlated mistakes, semantic failures, tokens, elapsed time and memory. Repeating deterministic greedy generation alone is not independent evidence. No voting or extra inference is enabled here.

Same-model self-verification is not independent linguistic evidence. Use frozen external dictionaries only for their demonstrated coverage and qualified human adjudication for semantic disputes. LoRA/fine-tuning is postponed: no trusted training set, independently frozen held-out split, accepted licensing pipeline or measured training-resource plan exists. Never train on disputed references or final test material. The audit/review pipeline comes first.
