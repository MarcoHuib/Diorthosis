# Phase 4 Dutch diagnostic-source investigation

Checked 2026-10-21 using public upstream descriptions; no dataset files, weights, libraries or tokenizers were downloaded or redistributed. This is a suitability/provenance screen, not a legal approval or human validation of Diorthosis fixtures.

[BLiMP-NL paper, Suijkerbuijk et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.cl-4.6/) describes 8,400 Dutch grammatical/ungrammatical sentence pairs across 84 paradigms and 22 syntactic phenomena, with manual construction/validation and acceptability/reading-time data for a subset. [Radboud's project page](https://www.ru.nl/en/research/research-projects/blimp-nl) confirms its contrastive grammatical-evaluation purpose. It is a useful potential diagnostic of syntax and sensitivity to grammatical contrasts. Pair preference is not correction quality: it supplies neither realistic multi-error proofreading passages nor necessary-edit/meaning/clean-preservation coverage. Do not convert minimal pairs into a representative end-to-end proofreading score.

The [Hugging Face mirror card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/juletxara/blimp-nl) declares CC-BY-4.0. That declaration permits commercial redistribution in principle with attribution and change identification, but this screen has not established an immutable upstream download revision, complete applicable notices, or the exact mirror-to-original transformation. Before importing a small separate diagnostic, verify those details against [the original Radboud data collection](https://data.ru.nl/collections/ru/cls/blimp-nl_dsc_550), preserve authors/citation/license and hashes, document any transformation and keep it outside the fictional proofreading panel. Public exposure also prevents calling it unseen. No BLiMP-NL labels are transferred as human validation of Diorthosis data.

[Kruijsbergen et al., Dutch L1/L2 writing error detection](https://clinjournal.org/clinj/article/view/179) reports more realistic annotated writing products, but explicitly describes its datasets as proprietary. The article's availability does not grant redistribution rights to that corpus. It is not imported.

The [GitHub Typo Corpus upstream README](https://github.com/mhagiwara/github-typo-corpus) assigns the individual texts/commits the originating repositories' license terms. Multilingual real-world typo corrections could be valuable, but importing a sample requires individual provenance/license checking and context review; commit messages/diffs may contain private or identifying content and valid code changes rather than proofreading repairs. It is deferred.

The Phase 4 panel therefore uses 200 original fictional passages under the project's MIT terms for any copyrightable contribution. There are no copied external passages, employer documents or personal source records. All annotation authority remains AI-generated/preparer-inspected, with zero independent AI-reviewed or human-validated labels at freeze. Exact prior fixture matching and internal character-similarity screening are recorded in `benchmarks/phase4/v1/overlap-audit.json`; these are screening checks, not proof of independent linguistic origins. The author knows prior failure categories. Ten development, seventy selection and twenty reserved confirmation passages per language have fixed identities before inference. Prompt examples never use selection or confirmation text. Reserved means inference-unseen until one locked comparison, not independently sealed or author-unseen.
